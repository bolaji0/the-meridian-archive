The Meridian Engine

A machine that remembers the future. A woman determined to forget it.

Mara Vey’s first novel follows a cartographer through a city whose streets are being erased by memories that have not happened yet.

01 / The story

At the edge of the salt plain, a city wakes each morning with one fewer street. Its inhabitants call this progress. Cartographer Iona Vale calls it a crime. When a map begins to show tomorrow’s disappearances, she travels beneath the city to find the machine that dreams them into being.

“A machine that remembers the future. A woman determined to forget it.”

02 / Opening passage
Original concept text

Chapter one.
The missing street

The first street disappeared on a Tuesday. Iona remembered this because Tuesday was the day she sharpened her pencils, and because there was no longer a shop from which to buy them.

She stood at the intersection of Bell and Orison and looked at the blank wall where a doorway ought to have been. The wall was old. That was the part she could not forgive. The city had not merely taken the street; it had arranged to have never possessed it.

On her map, the shop remained. A square no larger than her thumbnail, crosshatched in black. She pressed a finger to the paper and felt, absurdly, that she was keeping someone alive.

End of sample
